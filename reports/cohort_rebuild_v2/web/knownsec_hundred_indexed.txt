L35: # Preface
L36: 
L37: On the evening of March 15 Beijing time, Knownsec Blockchain Lab monitored that both Knownsec Blockchain Lab and Agave, the lending-like protocols on the Gnosis chain, had suffered a lightning loan attack, including Agave, a branch of AAVE, and Hundred Finance, a branch of Compound. the protocol lost over $11 million. Project parties have now suspended hundreds of their markets.
L38: 
L39: Knownsec Blockchain Lab was the first to track this incident and analyze it.
L40:

L62: ## Attack process
L63: 
L64: Since the Hundred Finance and Agave attack processes and techniques are similar, we use Hundred Finance for our analysis.
L65: 
L66: Hundred Finance attack invocation process
L67: 
L68: 1.The attacker uses contracts to lend WXDAI and USDC in three pools using lightning loans
L69: 
L70: 2.A portion of the USDC lent in the WETH pool pledge
L71: 
L72: 3.Use re-entry to leverage a pledge and over-lend funds in the pool
L73: 
L74: 4.Repeat the pledge-re-entry lending step to over-lend the other two pool assets
L75: 
L76: 5. Returning the flash loan
L77: 6. Profit transfer
L78: ## Vulnerability details
L79: 
L80: The root cause of this problem is due to a callback (callAfterTransfer function) implemented in the official bridge token xDAI contract on the Gnosis chain for the to address
L81: 
L82: The contract is implemented： athttps://blockscout.com/xdai/mainnet/address/0xf8D1677c8a0c961938bf2f9aDc3F3CFDA759A9d9/contracts
L83: This callback would have directly led to the creation of the reentry vulnerability, and because the check-validate-interaction (another way to prevent reentry) was not strictly followed in the Compound version of the Hundred Finance team fork, which in turn led to the total debits being updated later than the reentry implementation. Ultimately the attacker was able to overborrow.
L84: Similarly, for the Agave project side, an attacker using the internal call to the liquidateCall function will call the callAfterTransfer method of xDAI to make a callback, ultimately resulting in the attacker being able to debit again.

L89: # Summary
L90: 
L91: The two lending projects that suffered from this attack caused the problem due to the wrong introduction of the reentrant token and the failure of the project’s fork code to use the check-validate-interact pattern.
L92: 
L93: We emphasize that the citation of a good project must be based on sufficient understanding and rigorous development, especially the existence of differentiated parts, do not lose sight of the end because of a small error leads to the total loss of the project.
L94: Recently, various types of contract vulnerability security incidents have occurred frequently, contract audits, risk control measures, contingency plans, etc. are necessary to effectively implement.
